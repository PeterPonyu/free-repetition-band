\begin{abstract}
In language-model pretraining, a widely used data-reuse heuristic holds that repeating
the training corpus is ``nearly free'' for about four epochs. Whether this threshold
is a universal property of repeated training or merely the capacity-to-entropy ratio
at which it was first measured has not been tested, because capacity and entropy are
confounded in natural-language pretraining. We test it on synthetic Markov corpora
whose capacity and entropy are dialed independently: 218 training runs at a fixed
20M-token budget, five capacities ($2.5$M--$57$M parameters) and three entropies. A
linear reading of the capacity--entropy-artifact account predicts the free-epoch
threshold should move by ${\sim}100\times$ across the grid; it does not move (a
logarithmic reading predicts a sub-resolution shift and is not excluded). The free
zone instead reduces to a bounded $4$--$10$-epoch band, and a memorization-clock
account explains why the band lands near four independently of the ratio. The band's
collapse is accompanied by copied-canary memorization: an exposure-matched control
shows the coincidence of memorization and decay onset is an artifact of the
oversampled probe convention, while the band itself is invariant to that convention
($R_{\mathrm{free}}$, the largest repetition count whose median excess stays below
threshold, is identical in $9/9$ cells). The structure reproduces on a
context-free grammar and survives an optimizer swap to Muon. On real bytes,
WikiText-103 gives $R_{\mathrm{free}}{=}4$ across five seeds and three disjoint corpus
draws; a source-code corpus contracts the band to $2$ and returns to $4$ after
near-duplicate removal, so duplication-driven effective repetition, not byte-level
predictability, drives the contraction. The rule needs no scale-specific mechanism:
it is memorization onset, bounded.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} repeated data; data-constrained training;
memorization onset; controlled falsification; scaling laws; generalization

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Training large language models is data-hungry: empirical scaling laws show that model
performance improves as a power law in data volume \citep{kaplan2020scalinglaws}, and
compute-optimal recipes (``Chinchilla'' rules) demand token counts that scale linearly
with parameter count \citep{hoffmann2022chinchilla}. In data-constrained settings---where
unique tokens are scarce relative to the compute budget---practitioners inevitably repeat
the training corpus \citep{xue2023repeat}. \citet{muennighoff2023scaling} quantified the cost of this repetition
at large-language-model (LLM) scale, establishing a now-standard heuristic: repeating the data is
\emph{nearly free} for approximately four epochs, after which additional repetition yields
rapidly diminishing---eventually negative---returns. This $\approx 4$-epoch constant has
become a practical rule-of-thumb for data-reuse budgets. Yet the constant was measured in
a single regime (large language models, natural-language corpora with a particular
entropy-to-capacity ratio), and it is unclear whether it is universal or merely the value
the threshold takes at that regime's specific capacity-to-entropy ratio. The published
measurements cluster almost entirely at LLM scale
(Figure~\ref{fig:landscape}, Table~\ref{tab:repeat-landscape}), and current extensions
refine the repetition penalty within that same regime \citep{lovelace2026prescriptive}.

\paragraph{The capacity/entropy-artifact hypothesis.}
A natural alternative is that the ``free epochs'' threshold is a function of
how much the model's capacity exceeds the data's entropy---capacity-based accounts of
memorization quantify exactly such a budget \citep{morris2025capacity}. If so, the threshold should shift
by orders of magnitude as that ratio is varied across an experiment. The large-scale study
\citep{muennighoff2023scaling} could not test this because capacity and entropy are
confounded in natural-language training. An independent line of work
\citep{hernandez2022repeateddata} further predicts non-monotone (double-descent
\citep{nakkiran2020double}) loss curves under some repetition regimes. These hypotheses
jointly demand a controlled testbed where entropy and capacity are independently tunable.

\paragraph{This paper.}
We ask how many times a fixed corpus can be reused before its value collapses, on
synthetic Markov corpora with construction-tunable entropy---sweeping corpus entropy
and model capacity independently. Stated precisely once: our models are
$150$--$3{,}500\times$ smaller in parameter count ($2.5$M--$57$M vs.\ the
$8.7$B-parameter anchor) and our token budgets $18{,}000$--$45{,}000\times$ smaller
($20$--$50$M vs.\ $900$B tokens) than the anchor study of
\citet{muennighoff2023scaling}; elsewhere we describe this gap generically as
``orders of magnitude'' below language-model scale.\footnote{This is the only data-constrained-pretraining study in this line of
work: it asks how many times training tokens can be reused before their value
collapses, a repeated-data question that shares no phenomenon (repetition value decay
vs.\ delayed generalisation), no testbed (Markov corpora and WikiText bytes vs.\
modular arithmetic), and no citation base (repeated-data scaling and memorization vs.\
grokking and optimizers) with adjacent small-scale training-dynamics studies.} We
label four preregistered claims P1--P4 (P = prediction/probe). Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We show the free-epoch threshold does not shift with the capacity-to-entropy
        ratio, falsifying the linear-ratio form of the capacity--entropy-artifact
        account (P2; we state that form explicitly as the strongest testable member
        of the capacity-ratio family, since no published account commits to one, and
        a logarithmic form predicts a sub-resolution shift and is not
        excluded), and that the free zone is instead a bounded $4$--$10$ epoch band
        (P1)---across $>10\times$ entropy and ${\sim}23\times$ capacity
        ($2.5$M--$57$M params) at 20M-token
        scale (218 distinct training runs: nine core cells with full epoch-grid
        coverage at 3 seeds, a twelve-run boundary seed audit, and a 44-run
        xl/xxl capacity extension; the full $>10\times$ entropy factorial is
        core-grid, xl adds all three entropies, xxl the medium one).
  \item We show that repetition decay onset coincides with copied-canary memorization
        onset in 8 of 9 cells, an onset coincidence consistent with the memorization
        account \citep{tirumala2022memorization} (P3), and give a memorization-clock
        heuristic for why the band lands near four independently of the ratio.
  \item We provide a grid-resolution power argument showing the epoch grid is
        1--2 orders of magnitude finer than the linear account's predicted
        ${\sim}100\times$ shift, making that falsification a large-effect-size result
        rather than an underpowered null---while stating explicitly which alternative
        (logarithmic growth) the grid cannot resolve (\S\ref{sec:p2}).
  \item We extend the test to real bytes in two domains with corpus-level
        replication: WikiText-103 gives $R_{\mathrm{free}}=4$ in five seeds and three
        disjoint corpus draws. A source-code corpus ($1.8$ vs $2.8$ gzip bits/byte)
        separates two mechanisms: as collected it contracts the band to
        $R_{\mathrm{free}}=2$ ($5/5$), and near-duplicate removal restores
        $R_{\mathrm{free}}=4$---so the contraction is effective repetition from
        duplicated files, a practically important hazard of real code corpora, while
        the ratio account's predicted extension never appears. Canary probes ported to
        real bytes place memorization onset at or before decay onset in both domains
        (\S\ref{sec:scale}). A 48-run replication at $30$M--$57$M parameters keeps
        FineWeb-Edu inside the band ($R_{\mathrm{free}}=6$ and $4$, both threshold
        conventions within one rung) and reproduces the code contraction
        ($R_{\mathrm{free}}=1$--$2$ without near-duplicate removal), where the
        planted-canary clock lags decay at one capacity---an effective-repetition
        signature (\S\ref{sec:realscale}).
  \item We resolve the post-threshold decay shape (P4) on a fine epoch grid in four
        cells: excess grows super-linearly (roughly quadratic to cubic,
        cell-dependent; we show why a single decay exponent is not identified), and at
        half-octave resolution the memorization crossing leads or coincides with---and
        never lags---the decay onset, an ordering that holds in all nine cells once a
        30-run revision fine grid measures the remaining cells' crossings, and that
        is robust to halving, though not doubling, the canary detection threshold
        (\S\ref{sec:p3}, \S\ref{sec:p4}).
\end{enumerate}
This positions the work as a controlled falsification: the bounded $4$--$10$ band
holds across an order-of-magnitude span in entropy and a ${\sim}23\times$ span in
capacity, while exact thresholds and LLM-scale transfer remain open
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Relation to toy-model science.}
Our approach follows a tradition of using small, controlled testbeds to isolate mechanisms
that are confounded at scale. Grokking was first characterized on small algorithmic datasets \citep{power2022grokking}
and later shown to generalise beyond them \citep{liu2023omnigrok}; small-scale proxies have
also been used to study large-scale transformer training instabilities
\citep{wortsman2023smallscale}. We use Markov corpora to give the repetition law a
controlled stress-test with independently tunable variables that natural-language corpora
cannot provide. The scientific logic is
falsificationist: a ${\sim}100\times$ shift predicted by the linear-ratio account, with
no sign of it in the grid, is a strong negative result regardless of the absolute scale. Figure~\ref{fig:scheme} summarizes the design and findings. This reconciliation
register---explaining a phenomenon by an established mechanism at controlled scale
rather than positing a new one---follows recent Neurocomputing work reconciling
grokking with statistical-learning theory \citep{neucom2026grokking}.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:setup}

We train decoder transformers with a full-sequence next-token objective on synthetic
Markov corpora \citep{bietti2023birth} whose entropy is set by construction
(transition-matrix concentration). The
core sweep fixes the token budget at $B = 20$M and varies the number of unique tokens $U$
so that the epoch count $n = B/U \in \{1, 2, 4, 10, 20, 40\}$, crossed with capacity
$\{\text{small}, \text{medium}, \text{large}\}$ and entropy $\{\text{low}, \text{medium},
\text{high}\}$, under AdamW with 3 core seeds. Every run in the study uses AdamW at
weight decay $0.01$; unlike the learning rate, which is swept over a $10\times$ span
in the med/med cell (\S\ref{sec:limitations}), weight decay is held at this single
value throughout and is not varied, so all reported results are single-weight-decay
results. A follow-up seed audit adds seeds 3--4 for
the small/low and small/med boundary cells at $n\in\{4,10,20\}$; fresh-budget controls at
$\{5\text{M}, 10\text{M}, 40\text{M}\}$ provide the no-repetition reference. A revision
round adds two further arms at the same budget: a 30-run fine-grid extension
($n\in\{6,8\}$, three fresh seeds per point, seeds 20--22) covering the five cells
outside the fine grid of \S\ref{sec:p4}, and a twelve-seed audit (seeds 20--31) of the
med/low boundary rung at $n{=}10$ (Appendix~\ref{app:bootstrap}); a separate 48-run
real-text replication at larger byte budgets is described in \S\ref{sec:realscale}. Probes are held-out
validation loss and a copied-canary memorization gap \citep{carlini2019secret}. We operationalize the law as
$\mathrm{excess}(n) = \mathrm{val\_loss}(n) - \mathrm{val\_loss}(\text{fresh})$ and define
$R_{\mathrm{free}}$ as the largest $n$ with median excess below $0.05$ nats---the number
of ``free'' epochs. For intuition, $0.05$ nats is ${\approx}0.072$ bits per token;
expressed as a memorized fraction of each corpus's entropy floor,
$\phi = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{free}}/H$, the same absolute tolerance is ${\approx}3\%$ of
the medium-entropy floor but ${\approx}13\%$ of the low-entropy floor and
${\approx}1.2\%$ of the high-entropy floor---a convention dependence we quantify
directly with a relative-threshold reanalysis (Table~\ref{tab:twoconv},
\S\ref{sec:p1}). The three capacity presets are small ($d{=}256$, $L{=}3$, ${\approx}2.5$M
params), medium ($d{=}384$, $L{=}3$, ${\approx}5.6$M params), and large ($d{=}448$, $L{=}4$,
${\approx}10$M params)---extended upward by the xl/xxl presets of \S\ref{sec:capxl} to a
total ${\sim}23\times$ span---the core three spanning a ${\sim}4\times$ range in parameter count and
${\sim}900$--$3{,}500\times$ smaller than the $8.7$B-parameter models of
\citet{muennighoff2023scaling} (at the core $20$M budget the anchor's $900$B-token
budget is ${\sim}45{,}000\times$ larger; \S\ref{sec:intro}). The three entropy presets
correspond to per-token entropy floors of $0.37$ nats (low), $1.76$ nats (medium), and
$4.10$ nats (high)---operationalized throughout as the fresh-data ($n{=}1$) validation
loss, i.e.\ the achieved compression floor of each construction rather than its analytic
Markov entropy---a ${>}10\times$ span underpinning the entropy-axis coverage claim in
P2 (\S\ref{sec:p2}). Each synthetic corpus is generated by a Markov chain over a
$256$-token alphabet whose successor distributions are constructed, per context, by
assigning Gaussian logits to a fixed number of allowed successor tokens (the
branching factor) and applying a softmax at a preset temperature; all other
successors have probability zero. The low-entropy preset uses an order-2 chain (the
two-token context is hashed to one of $256$ transition rows) with branching $2$ and
temperature $0.4$; the medium and high presets are order-1 with branching $8$ at
temperature $1.0$ and branching $64$ at temperature $2.0$. Training and validation
sequences are contiguous length-$128$ windows of a single sampled stream, with chunk
order reshuffled each epoch. Table~\ref{tab:entropy} places the exact per-token
entropy rate of each construction---computed from the transition law via its
stationary distribution (over token pairs for the order-2 preset)---beside the
model-based floors: the floors exceed the achievable analytic values by
$0.022$--$0.070$ nats, and the shortfall is capacity-dependent only in the
low-entropy column (where it is also largest relative to the floor,
$7$--$15\%$), which is why we quote achieved compression floors rather than
analytic entropies throughout. The analytic entropy span,
$4.038/0.344 \approx 11.7\times$, confirms the ${>}10\times$ entropy-axis coverage
independently of the trained models.

\paragraph{Canary memorization probe.}
Each run plants $8$ canary sequences of $128$ tokens, sampled from the same
generator law as the training corpus under a dedicated seed. Each canary is spliced
into the unique-data pool $4$ times at uniformly random positions, so the planted
mass is $4{,}096$ tokens ($0.02\%$--$0.8\%$ of the pool depending on $U$), and every
planted copy is revisited once per epoch: at epoch count $n$ each canary has
received $4n$ gradient exposures. The memorization gap is the mean full-sequence
next-token cross-entropy on a matched fresh bank---$8$ sequences of the same length
from the same generator under disjoint seeds, never trained on---minus the mean on
the canary bank, so a positive gap means the model assigns lower loss to the exact
seen sequences than to statistically identical unseen ones. Memorization onset is
the smallest $n$ at which the median gap exceeds
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{can}} = 0.10$ nats; a sensitivity reanalysis of this
threshold at $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{can}} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$ nats is
reported in \S\ref{sec:p3}. (Real-byte corpora have no exchangeable
fresh bank; \S\ref{sec:scale} states the growth-above-$n{=}1$ convention used
there.)
The $4\times$ plant rate is a detector-sensitivity choice, and it makes the probe's
exposure count $4n$ rather than $n$: the canary bank is oversampled relative to the
corpus by construction. This distinguishes two axes on which any onset can be read,
the nominal per-epoch axis on which the free-reuse rule and $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ are
both defined, and a gradient-exposure axis on which the planted canaries advance
$4\times$ faster than the corpus they are embedded in. A dedicated $324$-run control
that repeats the full grid at plant rate $1$ separates the two
(Fig.~\ref{fig:exposure-control}), and
\S\ref{sec:p3exposure} reports both axes for every cell; the free band is unchanged
by the plant rate, while the onset ordering is specific to the nominal axis under
the oversampled convention.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

We organise the results as four claims, P1--P4: the free zone is a bounded $4$--$10$
epoch band across capacity and entropy (P1); the linear-ratio account's predicted
${\sim}100\times$ threshold shift is absent (P2); repetition decay onset coincides with
memorization onset (P3);
and the post-threshold decay shape resolves to steep super-linear growth (P4).

\subsection{A bounded 4--10 free-epoch band, invariant to capacity and entropy (P1)}\label{sec:p1}

Across the full-coverage cells, excess validation loss stays below $0.05$ nats through
$n = 4$ and then either breaks at $n = 10$ (e.g.\ med/med, absolute validation loss:
$1.764 \to 1.767 \to 1.778$ for $n = 1,2,4$---excess $0.003$/$0.014$ over the $n{=}1$
baseline---then $1.900 \to 3.409 \to 5.671$ for $n = 10,20,40$) or, in the audited
small/low and small/med cells, remains barely free at $n=10$ (excess $0.0498$) before
breaking at $n=20$. Thus $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ is a $4$--$10$ band rather than a point
constant (Table~\ref{tab:cells}). Excess is monotone in $n$---no repeated-data double-descent
\citep{hernandez2022repeateddata} appeared, so the non-monotone-decay falsifier did not
fire (Fig.~\ref{fig:repeat}).
The $4$--$10$ band is threshold-sensitive: at $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{free}}{=}0.045$ nats
the two boundary cells exceed the threshold and $R_{\mathrm{free}}=4$ everywhere; at
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{free}}{=}0.10$ nats the upper edge of the band expands
(Table~\ref{tab:cells}). We report $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ as a threshold decision rather
than a point estimate; a seed-level bootstrap audit of the two boundary cells at a
tripled seed budget (15 seeds per cell; Appendix~\ref{app:bootstrap}) confirms this is
forced, not cautious: the 95\% confidence intervals on their $n{=}10$ excess
($[0.029,0.057]$ and $[0.043,0.062]$ nats) narrow onto the $0.05$-nat threshold rather
than away from it, with bootstrap exceedance probabilities of $0.15$ and $0.42$.
A revision fine-grid extension subsequently measured each remaining cell's crossing
directly (Table~\ref{tab:crossings}, \S\ref{sec:p4}): the interpolated crossings run
from $n^\star = 5.04$ to $10.34$ and are dispersed rather than concentrated, with
seven inside the band and the two small-capacity cells just above its upper edge.
The band edges are thus rounded measured crossings rather than a common crossing
quantized by the coarse grid's $2.5\times$ spacing, subject to the design limit
noted in \S\ref{sec:p4} that the coarse grid had already confined each cell's
crossing before the fine rungs were added.

\paragraph{Absolute vs.\ relative threshold conventions.}\label{par:twoconv}
The declared $0.05$-nat criterion is absolute, so as a fraction of each cell's
entropy floor it is a looser tolerance at low entropy ($0.05/0.37 \approx 13\%$)
than at high ($0.05/4.10 \approx 1.2\%$). To test whether the band depends on that
convention, we recompute every cell under a relative criterion
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cell}} = \phi\, H_{\mathrm{cell}}$, with $H_{\mathrm{cell}}$
the cell's fresh-data floor and $\phi = 0.05/H_{\mathrm{med/med}} = 0.028$
calibrated so the reference cell keeps its absolute verdict. Seven of nine cells
keep their verdict unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:twoconv}). The two that change are the
small-capacity entropy extremes, and they move in opposite directions, as a pure
rescaling requires: small/low tightens ($R_{\mathrm{free}} = 10 \to 2$; its $n{=}4$
excess of $0.0125$ nats exceeds the $0.0115$-nat relative threshold by $0.001$
nats---a threshold-edge call at the same razor margin as the absolute boundary
cells), while small/high loosens ($R_{\mathrm{free}} = 4 \to 10$; $n{=}10$ excess
$0.090 < 0.116$). Under the relative convention the band's numeric label is
therefore $2$--$10$ rather than $4$--$10$, with the medium- and large-capacity rows
pinned at $R_{\mathrm{free}} = 4$ under both conventions. What is
convention-independent: the bounded band, the absence of any monotone trend in
entropy or capacity, and the absence of the linear-ratio account's predicted
${\sim}100\times$ shift---the largest convention-induced movement is one grid rung,
confined to the small-capacity corner. What is convention-dependent: the specific

\subsection{Not a linear capacity/entropy artifact (P2)}\label{sec:p2}

The artifact hypothesis, in the form this grid is built to test, holds that the
free-epoch threshold tracks the capacity-to-entropy ratio proportionally: if
$R_{\mathrm{free}}$ were simply ``the number of epochs at which capacity begins to exceed
what the data's entropy can fill,'' it should scale roughly linearly with that ratio. We
state this linear form explicitly because no published account commits to one:
capacity-accounting results \citep{morris2025capacity} quantify a memorization budget in
bits but do not specify how a free-repetition threshold should scale with it, and the one
explicit scaling claim in the literature is logarithmic \citep{yan2025repeatmore}. The
linear form is the strongest testable member of the capacity-ratio family, and the grid
should be read as constraining that family---excluding every member that predicts a
shift of one grid rung (${\geq}2.5\times$) or more across the $44\times$ ratio
span---rather than as falsifying a specific published claim. Our
grid moves the ratio by a factor of ${\approx}44$ (an $11\times$ span of measured corpus
entropy, $0.37$--$4.10$ nats, crossed with a $3.9\times$ span of parameters,
$2.5$--$9.9$M), so the linear reading predicts a shift of order $10$--$100\times$. We do
not observe any such shift: $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ stays in the $4$--$10$ range across all
nine cells, including the full large-capacity row (\S\ref{sec:largecompletion}), and the
natural-text tests add an out-of-family bridge (Fig.~\ref{fig:grid}). One honest scope
limit: the literature also contains a logarithmic reading---\citet{yan2025repeatmore}
argue the tolerable repetition count grows as $\Theta(\log N)$ with data size---and over
this grid's $40\times$ span of unique tokens a logarithmic account predicts a movement of
at most one grid rung, below our $2.5\times$-per-rung resolution. The grid therefore
falsifies the linear-ratio form of the artifact account and cannot separate the
bounded-universal reading from a slow logarithmic growth; the two accounts diverge only
at spans far larger than any controlled testbed, which is exactly where the
memorization-onset mechanism (P3) provides the discriminating evidence instead.

\paragraph{Grid-resolution power argument.}\label{par:powerarg}
The null result above is a properly powered falsification of the linear-ratio
alternative, not an underpowered failure-to-find---and, equally, it is powered against
that alternative alone; the logarithmic account noted above predicts a
sub-rung movement this grid cannot detect, and we do not claim to falsify it. The epoch
grid $n \in \{1, 2, 4, 10, 20, 40\}$ has a
multiplicative step size of $\approx 2.5\times$ per rung.  The linear-ratio hypothesis predicts
a $\sim 100\times$ shift of $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ with the capacity/entropy ratio; even a
conservative $10\times$ upward shift from the observed base of $R_{\mathrm{free}} \approx 4$
would require $R_{\mathrm{free}} \approx 40$---the highest rung on the grid, which was
measured in every cell and never reached.  A full $100\times$ shift would require
$R_{\mathrm{free}} \approx 400$ epochs, entirely off the grid; such an effect would
manifest as ``still free at $n = 40$,'' which does not occur in any cell.  The predicted
effect size ($\geq 10\times$) is therefore 1--2 orders of magnitude larger than the grid's
detection floor (${\approx}2.5\times$ per step), making this a large-effect-size
falsification robust to the grid's coarseness:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{2.5\times}_{\text{grid step (detectable)}}
  \;\ll\;
  \underbrace{10\text{--}100\times}_{\text{predicted shift}}
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  R_{\mathrm{free}}^{\text{pred}} \approx 40\text{--}400,
  \qquad
  R_{\mathrm{free}}^{\text{obs}} = 4\text{--}10 \ \ ({\leq}2.5\times).
  \label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
The observed spread across $>10\times$
entropy and $\sim 4\times$ capacity is $4$--$10$ ($\leq 2.5\times$, i.e.\ $\leq 1$ grid
step), confirming the absence of the predicted shift of Eq.~\eqref{eq:power}; the xl/xxl extension
(\S\ref{sec:capxl}) holds $R_{\mathrm{free}}=4$ out to a ${\sim}23\times$ span.

\subsection{The decay onset coincides with memorization onset (P3)}\label{sec:p3}

On the nominal per-epoch axis, and under the $4\times$ plant convention of
\S\ref{sec:setup}, the onset of repetition damage (decay onset,
$\delta_{\mathrm{decay}}$, in
Table~\ref{tab:cells}) coincides with the onset of canary memorization (the
copied-canary gap) in 8 of 9 cells (Table~\ref{tab:cells}, ``Coincide'' column).
Both scope qualifiers are load-bearing and are established quantitatively in
\S\ref{sec:p3exposure}, which repeats the entire grid at plant rate $1$.
Fresh-budget controls---where the same
token count is supplied as unique data---show no comparable gap, ruling out a
sampling/estimation artifact: the onset is accompanied by repetition-driven memorization
rather than a budget effect---an observational coincidence on a coarse grid that we do not
interpret as proof that memorization causes the damage.
The tally's evidential weight must also be scoped against the grid geometry: both
onsets are quantized to a $2.5\times$-spaced grid, and the post-threshold cliff spans
only one to two grid intervals in most cells, so an unrelated monotone quantity
forced to cross somewhere within the cliff would land on the decay-onset rung with
probability $0.23$--$0.57$ per cell and would reproduce a tally of ${\geq}8/9$ with
probability ${\approx}0.009$ (under uniform placement on the grid's five candidate
rungs, ${\approx}2\times10^{-5}$); correlation from the shared repetition stress
could inflate the tally further. The coarse-grid coincidence is therefore
suggestive rather than decisive on its own; the informative evidence for the onset
ordering is the half-octave fine grid of \S\ref{sec:p4}, where memorization leads
or coincides with---and never lags---decay in all nine cells (four in the original
fine grid, five in the revision extension of Table~\ref{tab:crossings}), together with
the seed-robust small/low dissociation (Appendix~\ref{app:bootstrap}).

\paragraph{Canary-threshold sensitivity.}\label{par:cansens}
The $0.10$-nat canary criterion is itself a convention, so we recompute every
cell's memorization onset at $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{can}} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$
nats on the pooled fine-grid gap curves, holding decay onset at the declared
$0.05$-nat excess criterion. The leads-or-coincides tally is robust to halving the
threshold but not to doubling it: $9/9$ cells at both $0.05$ and $0.10$ nats, but
$3/9$ at $0.20$ nats, where the stricter detector fires one to two rungs later
along the same rising gap curve and postdates decay onset in six cells. The
asymmetry is mechanical rather than evidential---any sufficiently strict detector
eventually postdates a fixed decay criterion---but it bounds what the ordering
claim means: the median canary gap at each cell's decay-onset rung is
$0.11$--$0.24$ nats, so the threshold-robust statement is that a ${\geq}0.10$-nat
memorization signal is already present at or before decay onset in all nine cells,
not that a threshold-free ``memorization event'' precedes a threshold-free
``decay event.'' The exact ceiling is worth stating rather than leaving implicit
in that range. Because a cell's memorization onset precedes its decay onset
exactly when the canary gap reaches $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{can}}$ at or before the
decay-onset rung, the tally holds at $9/9$ for every
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{can}} \leq 0.1119$ nats and falls to $8/9$ immediately
above it. The binding cell is small/med, whose gap first reaches $0.1119$ at
$n{=}12$, its own decay-onset rung. The declared $0.10$-nat criterion therefore
carries $11.9\%$ headroom, which is real but not large, and the ordering claim
should be read as scoped to detectors at least that sensitive. Collecting these
qualifiers: the published leads-or-coincides ordering holds under three conditions
that must all be met together---the nominal per-epoch axis rather than the
gradient-exposure axis, the $4\times$ canary plant rate rather than an
exposure-matched rate of $1$, and a canary criterion at or below $0.1119$
nats---and it fails outside any one of them, whereas $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ and the
bounded band survive all three variations unchanged. The ordering is also optimizer-scoped: under Muon the
medium-capacity cell inverts it---decay onset ($n{=}20$) precedes
canary-threshold memorization onset ($n{=}40$) by one grid rung
(\S\ref{sec:pcfgmuon})---so leads-or-coincides is established for AdamW, and the
Muon inversion marks an explicit boundary of the memorization-clock account taken
up in \S\ref{sec:memclock}.

\paragraph{An exposure-matched control separates the two axes.}\label{sec:p3exposure}
The $4\times$ plant rate makes the canary bank advance four times faster than the
corpus in gradient exposures, so the ordering above is stated on an axis where the
probe is oversampled relative to the quantity it is used to explain. A dedicated
$324$-run control isolates the plant rate: the full $3\times3$ grid is re-run at
plant rate $1$ and at plant rate $4$ on the same three fresh seeds ($201$--$203$),
the same epoch grid $n\in\{1,2,4,10,20,40\}$, the same generator law, and the same
two criteria, so the plant rate is the only quantity that differs between the two
arms (Table~\ref{tab:exposure}).
Two results separate cleanly. The free band does not move: $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ is
identical between the two plant rates in all nine cells, so neither the $4$--$10$
band nor the absence of the ratio account's predicted ${\sim}100\times$ shift
depends on the probe convention at all. The ordering does move: at plant rate $4$
the two onsets coincide in $9/9$ cells of this control, while at the
exposure-matched rate $1$ the canary clock reads later than decay in $6$ of $9$
cells, memorization onset moving from $n{=}10$ to $n{=}20$ against a decay onset
that stays at $n{=}10$. The three cells that still coincide (large/high, med/high,
small/med) are those whose canary gap is already well clear of the criterion at the
decay rung. In the six lagging cells the rate-$1$ canary gap at the decay rung is
$0.030$--$0.070$ nats, $30$--$70\%$ below the $0.10$-nat criterion, so no
re-thresholding recovers coincidence in them without lowering the detector to a
level that fires on the $n\leq4$ rungs where no decay has occurred.
On the gradient-exposure axis the reading is starker: scoring both arms in exposures
rather than epochs puts memorization onset after decay onset in $9/9$ cells,
including the published plant-rate-$4$ runs.
Two disclosures attach to this control. Its plant-rate-$4$ arm gives $9/9$
coincidence where Table~\ref{tab:cells} reports $8/9$, because on these three fresh
seeds small/low places decay onset at $n{=}10$ rather than $n{=}20$; that cell is
the threshold-edge cell whose seed sensitivity Appendix~\ref{app:bootstrap}
documents, so the control's r$4$ arm is a matched-seed comparison against its own
r$1$ arm rather than a reproduction of the archived table. And the direction of the
effect is the one a fixed-clock account predicts---fewer exposures per epoch move a
fixed-exposure detector to a later epoch---so the control bounds the scope of the
ordering claim without disturbing the band. The P2 falsification is untouched, since
it rests on the absence of the ratio account's predicted shift in
$R_{\mathrm{free}}$ and not on the onset ordering.

\subsection{The post-threshold decay shape (P4)}\label{sec:p4}

A fine epoch grid ($n\in\{6,8,12,16\}$, three to five seeds per point, interleaved with
the coarse grid's $n\in\{2,4,10,20,40\}$) resolves the shape that the coarse grid left
open, in four cells spanning the design's corners: med/med, large/high, small/high, and
large/low. In med/med the median excess runs $0.003$, $0.014$, $0.027$, $0.059$,
$0.136$, $0.255$, $0.724$, $1.65$, $3.91$ nats across $n=2$--$40$: the $0.05$-nat
threshold is crossed between $n=6$ and $n=8$, and beyond threshold the excess grows
steeply and smoothly. Three features matter. First, the shape is not cliff-like in any
of the four cells: post-threshold growth is super-linear everywhere, so the practical
cost of overshooting the band by one grid step is bounded but compounding. Second, we
deliberately do not report a decay exponent as a result: a log--log fit over the
post-threshold points gives slopes of $2.9$, $2.7$, $2.4$, and $1.7$ in the four cells,
and the fitted value moves by up to a full unit under defensible changes of fit window
(the fit start is itself defined by a threshold crossing), so the honest summary is
``roughly quadratic-to-cubic growth, cell-dependent,'' not a universal power law.
Third, the canary memorization gap crosses its own $0.10$-nat threshold either in the
same fine-grid interval as the decay onset or one interval before it, in all four
cells---the onset order is never inverted (memorization never lags decay), which is the
ordering a memorization-gated account requires and which \S\ref{sec:p3}'s coarse-grid
coincidence could not resolve. Per-seed spread is small throughout (three to five seeds
per point; full per-point ranges in the archived logs).

\paragraph{A revision extension measures every cell's crossing.}\label{par:crossings}
The four-cell fine grid leaves open whether the remaining cells' onsets are quantization
of the coarse grid's $2.5\times$ spacing. A 30-run extension adds the $n\in\{6,8\}$
rungs to the five cells not yet covered (med/high, large/med, med/low, small/low,
small/med; three fresh seeds per point, seeds 20--22, 20M-token budget), so every cell
of the core grid now has a measured threshold crossing (Table~\ref{tab:crossings}). The
crossings fan out rather than piling on one rung, occupying
four distinct half-octave brackets: med/high and large/high cross between $n{=}4$ and
$6$ (med/high median excess $0.0512$ at $n{=}6$, a threshold-edge call: per-seed range
$0.045$--$0.054$); med/med and large/med between $n{=}6$ and $8$ ($0.0394 \to 0.0973$
for large/med); med/low, large/low, and small/high between $n{=}8$ and $10$ (med/low
$0.0484 \to 0.0776$, with the $n{=}8$ rung itself threshold-edge); and the two
small-capacity boundary cells remain below threshold at $n{=}8$ ($0.034$ and $0.026$ on
the fresh seeds, matching the archived ten-seed medians of $0.040$ and $0.026$) before
crossing between $n{=}10$ and $12$ (Appendix~\ref{app:bootstrap}).

Interpolating each crossing directly turns the spread from an impression into a
measurement. Solving $\mathrm{excess}(n^\star) = 0.05$ nats by log-linear
interpolation between the two rungs that bracket each cell's crossing, with a
run-level bootstrap over seeds, places the nine crossings from $n^\star = 5.04$
(large/high, 95\% CI $[4.80, 5.19]$) to $n^\star = 10.34$ (small/low,
$[9.14, 11.19]$), a $2.05\times$ spread. Twenty-six of the $36$ pairwise
comparisons have disjoint intervals, and no single crossing value is compatible
with all nine, since the largest lower bound, $9.14$, exceeds the smallest upper
bound, $5.19$. The crossings are therefore dispersed across the band's interior
rather than concentrated at a common value that the grid rounds to different
rungs. The two small-capacity cells cross at $n^\star = 10.34$ and $10.05$, just
above the band's declared upper edge, so the nine crossings span $5.0$ to $10.3$
rather than falling strictly within $4$--$10$.

Two limits bound what this establishes. First, the fine grid is a partial rather
than a complete answer to the grid-quantization objection, because the coarse
grid had already constrained every cell's crossing before any fine rung was run.
All nine cells were free at $n{=}4$, and seven were already broken at $n{=}10$,
so for those seven the crossing was pinned to $(4, 10]$ in advance and the added
rungs at $6$ and $8$ could only locate it inside an interval the coarse grid had
fixed; the remaining two cells were free at $n{=}10$ as well, which pinned their
crossings above $10$. What the fine grid measures is therefore the dispersion of
the crossings and their position within pre-fixed intervals, not independently
the location of the band. Second, three of the nine bracket assignments turn on
margins below $0.002$ nats and are close to coin flips. The share of bootstrap
replicates landing in the assigned bracket is $0.58$ for small/med, whose
$n{=}10$ excess falls $0.0008$ nats short of threshold, $0.62$ for med/low
($0.0016$ nats short at $n{=}8$) and $0.74$ for med/high ($0.0012$ nats over at
$n{=}6$), against $0.81$ to $1.00$ in the other six cells
(Table~\ref{tab:crossings}). We therefore treat the interpolated crossing points
and their intervals as the primary quantitative content of that table and retain
the bracket labels because the two-convention comparison of \S\ref{sec:p1} is
stated in them.

The
canary ordering extends with it: in the five newly covered cells the memorization
crossing again leads or coincides with---and never lags---the decay crossing (e.g.\
med/low canary gap $0.097 \to 0.146$ across $n{=}6 \to 8$ against a decay crossing
between $n{=}8$ and $10$), so the leads-or-coincides ordering is fine-grid-verified in
all nine cells. Under the relative convention of \S\ref{sec:p1} the crossings shift as
the rescaled thresholds require---low-entropy cells cross earlier under their tighter
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}$ and high-entropy cells later under their looser one---but
no coarse-grid $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ verdict of Table~\ref{tab:twoconv} changes under
either convention.

\subsection{A memorization-clock reading of the band}\label{sec:memclock}

The invariance of $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ across the grid (P2) together with its coincidence
with memorization onset (P3) admits a back-of-envelope reading. Write $U$ for the number
of unique tokens, $B$ for the token budget, $n=B/U$ for the epoch count, $H$ for the
per-token entropy floor, and $C$ for usable model capacity (bits), and define the
capacity-to-entropy ratio $\rho = C/(U H)$.

\emph{Assumptions.} (A1, non-binding capacity) in the free regime $\rho \gtrsim 1$, so the
model can fit the unique corpus without memorizing repeats. (A2, fixed memorization clock)
memorizing a specific length-$\ell$ sequence to the canary threshold takes a roughly fixed
number $k^\star$ of passes over that sequence, counted on the nominal per-epoch axis and
set by the optimizer's per-example memorization
time---an SGD implicit-bias quantity in the spirit of \citep{neucom2026sgdbias}---and, once
(A1) holds, independent of $C$ and $H$. (A3, floor crossing) excess validation loss stays
below $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{free}}$ until repeated surface statistics begin to be memorized.

The axis in (A2) is a restriction, and we state it before using it. Both
$R_{\mathrm{free}}$ and the free-reuse rule it tests are defined per epoch, so that is
the axis on which the heuristic is posed. Counted instead in gradient exposures, the
planted canaries reach the criterion at $k^\star \approx 40$ exposures under the
$4\times$ plant convention and $\approx 20$ under the exposure-matched control
(\S\ref{sec:p3exposure}), against $R_{\mathrm{free}} \approx 4$ epochs, a gap of
$5$--$10\times$. The numerical agreement below is therefore an agreement in epochs
only; it does not survive translation into exposures, and we do not read it as
identifying one mechanism common to both quantities.

\emph{Heuristic.} Under (A1)--(A3), and on the nominal axis, $R_{\mathrm{free}} \approx k^\star$, independent of
$\rho$ across the range where (A1) holds. The observed $k^\star \approx 4$ is then a property
of the memorization clock, not of the capacity-to-entropy ratio, so no ${\sim}100\times$
shift with $\rho$ is predicted---consistent with P2, though P2's falsification does not
depend on this reading: it rests on the measured absence of the ratio account's
predicted shift in $R_{\mathrm{free}}$, which stands whether or not the clock
interpretation is accepted. The clock constant itself is family- and
optimizer-dependent while remaining $\rho$-invariant within a construction: the probabilistic-context-free-grammar (PCFG)
family gives $k^\star\approx20$ and Muon shifts it ${\sim}2$--$2.5\times$
(Section~\ref{sec:pcfgmuon}). The residual drift to $R_{\mathrm{free}}=10$ in
the small-capacity, low-entropy corner is the boundary where (A1) tightens ($\rho$ falls
toward $1$): with less spare capacity the memorization clock runs \emph{slower},
\emph{lengthening} the free band---the opposite sign to the artifact account, which requires
$R_{\mathrm{free}}$ to \emph{grow with} $\rho$.

\emph{Floor calibration (order of magnitude).} Reading excess as a memorized fraction
$\phi$ times entropy, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{free}} \approx \phi\,H$, the declared $0.05$ nats
correspond to $\phi \approx \varepsilon_{\mathrm{free}}/H \approx 0.03$ at medium entropy
($H{=}1.76$)---a few-percent memorized-fraction tolerance, consistent with the sharp break at
$n{=}10$.

This is an analytic sketch, not a derivation, and after the exposure-matched control
it is best read as a heuristic stated on the nominal axis rather than as the
mechanism behind P2. Assumption (A2)---that $k^\star$ is
capacity- and entropy-invariant---is \emph{post-hoc consistent} with the grid (P2), not
independently derived: the grid is consistent with a capacity-invariant memorization clock,
but we do not prove one. Two further limits are now explicit. The $R_{\mathrm{free}}
\approx k^\star$ agreement is an epoch-unit agreement that becomes a $5$--$10\times$
disagreement in exposure units, so it is evidence that a per-epoch memorization
clock is a useful bookkeeping device for a per-epoch data-budget rule, not evidence
that a single fixed-exposure quantity sets both onsets. And the coincidence in (P3)
that motivated the reading is itself convention-scoped
(\S\ref{sec:p3exposure}). What the nominal axis retains is its operational
content: the question a data-budget rule has to answer is whether memorization has
appeared by the epoch at which reuse stops being free, and that question is posed
per epoch, with the $4\times$ plant rate buying detector sensitivity rather than
changing the epoch at which the corpus is reused. The $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{free}}\approx\phi H$ conversion is
back-of-envelope. The small-capacity sign argument, by contrast, checks out in the existing
logs: at every matched $(n,\text{entropy})$ point with $n\geq10$, the small-capacity median
canary gap is smaller than both larger capacities' ($18/18$ pairwise comparisons), and the
only onset-grade shift on the coarse $n$-grid (memorization onset $10\to20$) occurs in a
small-capacity cell---the memorization clock indeed runs slower, not faster, as $\rho$
falls toward $1$, the sign that separates the clock reading from the capacity/entropy-artifact
account. The Muon arm (\S\ref{sec:pcfgmuon}) marks a second, sharper boundary: at
medium capacity the onset ordering inverts (decay onset $n{=}20$ precedes
canary-threshold memorization onset $n{=}40$), so Muon's outward-shifted band is not
gated by canary-threshold memorization the way (A2)--(A3) describe. A slower clock
constant alone cannot produce this---rescaling $k^\star$ delays both onsets
together---so the clock reading as stated is AdamW-specific: under Muon either the
canary probe under-reads the relevant memorization or the decay onset has a
component the memorization gate does not control.

\subsection{Real-text bridge: the band holds on WikiText bytes and moves on code}\label{sec:scale}

As a bridge beyond synthetic Markov data, we repeat the test on real text. Using
WikiText-103 as a byte stream
(vocabulary 256, so the model is identical to the synthetic setup) with a larger
${\sim}5$M-parameter decoder and a $2.5\times$ larger budget ($B=50$M tokens), the band
holds unchanged: excess validation loss stays below $0.05$ nats through $n=4$
(median $0.019$, range $0.013$--$0.029$), breaks at $n=10$ (median $0.086$, range
$0.068$--$0.102$), and is severe by $n=40$ ($2.05$--$2.51$ in the two seeds run that
far), giving $R_{\mathrm{free}}=4$ in all five seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:e1scale}).
This small-scale real-text bridge is consistent with, but does not prove, scale robustness.
Three separate $10$M-token real-text audit seeds now recover the same decision boundary
($R_{\mathrm{free}}=4$): excess at $n=4$ is $0.0086/0.0079/0.0180$, while $n=10$
excess is $0.086/0.088/0.101$ and $n=20$ is $0.598/0.657/0.669$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:e1scale}d). The $0.1$-nat
threshold is sensitive at $n=10$ (two of three seeds would extend to $R_{\mathrm{free}}=10$),
so the paper keeps $0.05$ nats as the decision threshold and treats the real-text result as
a small bridge rather than an LLM-scale proof. A capacity bridge at small and large
real-text model sizes confirms the free band spans $R_{\mathrm{free}}=4$--10 across
capacities and thresholds, with $n=20$ consistently costly
(Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity-bridge}).

The replication level of these byte arms deserves precision: the five WikiText seeds
above rerun one fixed corpus draw (optimization noise only), so we additionally ran
three disjoint $52$M-byte WikiText shards; all three give $R_{\mathrm{free}}=4$
(excess at $n=4$: $-0.007$--$0.018$; at $n=10$: $0.051$--$0.078$), so the wiki verdict
is stable at the corpus-draw level as well.

The two accounts of the band make opposite predictions for a second, more predictable
real domain, so we ran one: source code, under the identical protocol
($50$M-token budget, medium capacity, the same epoch grid). Code is far more
compressible than encyclopedic prose ($1.8$ vs $2.8$ bits/byte under gzip-9 on matched
$20$MB samples), so the capacity-to-entropy ratio is higher and the ratio account
predicts the free zone should extend. What we find separates two mechanisms instead.
On the corpus as collected (exact-deduplicated Python source from a CPython
installation and its site-packages---which, like all real code corpora, retains
version-vendored near-duplicate files), the band contracts to $R_{\mathrm{free}}=2$ in
five of five seeds: excess $0.011$--$0.030$ at $n=2$, $0.058$--$0.072$ at $n=4$. But
after MinHash near-duplicate removal (Jaccard $\geq0.8$ on $32$-byte shingles;
compressibility unchanged at $1.83$ bits/byte), three disjoint corpus draws give
$R_{\mathrm{free}}=4$, $4$, and $2$---the third at the threshold edge (excess $0.0503$
at $n=4$; the other two at $0.036$ and $0.044$, versus WikiText's $0.014$--$0.018$).
The as-collected contraction is therefore driven by effective repetition from
near-duplicated files, not by byte-level predictability itself: duplication makes the
nominal epoch count understate the true reuse, and the free band shrinks accordingly.
Both readings matter. For practice, real code corpora carry exactly this duplication,
so their free band genuinely is shorter at face value---deduplication is what buys the
epochs back. For the theory contest, the ratio account's predicted extension fails to
appear under either treatment, while the deduplicated corpus sits measurably closer to
the threshold at $n=4$ than prose does, a residual earlier-pressure signal consistent
with, but not proving, the memorization account.

The canary probe also ports to real bytes, with one convention made explicit: real
canary and fresh banks are not statistically exchangeable the way synthetic ones are
(the two banks are different real byte sequences), so the memorization signal is the
growth of the gap above its $n{=}1$ level rather than its absolute value. Under that
convention, on WikiText the gap grows by $0.10$ nats between $n=2$ and $n=4$ and decay
onsets at $n=4$--$10$; on the as-collected code corpus the gap growth crosses the same
level between $n=2$ and $n=4$ while decay onsets at $n=2$--$4$. In both domains
memorization onset arrives at or before decay onset---the same never-inverted ordering
as the synthetic grid (\S\ref{sec:p4})---and both onsets shift down together on the
duplication-heavy corpus. (At $30$M parameters the code-corpus ordering does
invert; \S\ref{sec:realscale} reports and interprets that lag.) Remaining caveats
are recorded in \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Real-text replication at 30M--57M parameters}\label{sec:realscale}

The byte arms above top out near $5$M parameters, so their verdicts could still be
read as small-model accidents. A 48-run replication raises the real-text capacity
to the top of the study's span: byte-level decoders at $30.0$M and $57.2$M
parameters (the xl/xxl capacity presets of \S\ref{sec:capxl} instantiated at the
byte protocol's $256$-token context, hence marginally larger counts than the
synthetic arms' $29.9$M/$57.1$M), trained on document-disjoint
fixed byte budgets of $100$M and $200$M respectively, drawn from two corpora---
FineWeb-Edu educational web text, and an exact-deduplicated GitHub code collection
that, like the as-collected condition of \S\ref{sec:scale} and like real code
corpora generally, retains version-vendored near-duplicate files. The protocol is
otherwise unchanged: $U=B/n$ unique bytes repeated $n$ times at fixed budget, epoch
grids $n\in\{1,2,4,6,8,10,16\}$ (FineWeb) and $n\in\{1,2,4,10,16\}$ (code), two
fresh seeds per cell, AdamW, fresh validation on a held-out $2$M-byte document tail,
and eight $256$-byte canaries planted four times each ($4n$ exposures at epoch
count $n$) under the growth-above-$n{=}1$ gap convention stated in
\S\ref{sec:scale}. Table~\ref{tab:realscale} gives every verdict under both
threshold conventions.

at most one grid rung while its boundedness is not.

The code corpus contracts below the band at both capacities:
$R_{\mathrm{free}}=1$ at $30$M and $2$ at $57$M (both seeds each; $1$ under the
relative convention). This reproduces the as-collected code contraction of
\S\ref{sec:scale} ($R_{\mathrm{free}}=2$ at ${\sim}5$M) at $6$--$11\times$ the
parameters, on an independently collected corpus family. The reading offered
there---duplication-driven effective repetition, since exact deduplication leaves
the near-duplicate files that make the nominal epoch count understate true
reuse---predicts exactly this: no near-duplicate-removal arm was run at this
scale, so the present cells correspond to the as-collected condition, and the
band they show is the practically relevant face-value one. Whether MinHash
deduplication buys the epochs back at $30$--$57$M as it does at $5$M remains
untested here.

The onset ordering is capacity-robust on FineWeb and breaks, informatively, on
code. On FineWeb the memorization crossing leads decay at both capacities under
the declared $0.10$-nat criterion ($n=4$ vs $8$ at $30$M; $4$ vs $6$ at $57$M)
and never lags at any of the three thresholds (at $0.20$ nats the $57$M cell
coincides at $n=6$). On code/xxl the two onsets coincide at $n=4$. But on
code/xl decay onsets at $n=2$ while the planted-canary crossing arrives at
$n=4$ (both seeds)---the first observed lag in this study, at the $0.10$- and
$0.20$-nat criteria; at the halved $0.05$-nat criterion the growth crossing
moves to $n=2$ and the ordering returns to coincidence (and the code grid has
no rung between $2$ and $4$, so the lag is at most one coarse rung). We report
this as a genuine boundary rather than noise, and it is the boundary the
duplication account itself draws: the planted canary counts nominal repetition
($4n$ exposures), while on a duplication-heavy corpus decay is driven by
effective repetition of corpus-internal near-duplicates that the planted probe
does not track, so decay can front-run the probe precisely where duplication
understates the epoch count. The lead-or-coincide ordering should therefore be
read as a property of corpora whose repetition is nominal---it held in all nine
synthetic cells, on WikiText, and on FineWeb at both capacities---not as a
corpus-independent law.

Caveats: two seeds per cell (against $3$--$15$ on the synthetic grid), two corpus
families, no deduplication arm, a coarser code epoch grid, and a capacity ceiling
of $57$M---models at ${\sim}100$M parameters and above remain untested on real
text. Within those limits, the replication removes the two scoping attacks the
earlier bridges left open: the band and its onset ordering are not
Markov-family-specific, and on natural text they are not specific to
${\sim}5$M-parameter models either.

\subsection{Two further stress tests: a second synthetic family and an optimizer swap}\label{sec:pcfgmuon}

Two follow-up sweeps target two axes the core grid leaves untested: whether the band's
numeric value is specific to the Markov construction, and whether it is specific to
AdamW.

\paragraph{A second generative family (PCFG).}
We repeat the core protocol on a probabilistic context-free grammar (ambiguity
$0.25$; measured conditional entropy ${\approx}4.04$ bits at $n{=}1$---not
entropy-matched to the Markov medium-entropy preset (${\approx}2.54$ bits, i.e.\ the $1.76$-nat preset of Section~\ref{sec:setup}): the two
families' entropy labels are internal to each construction, not a matched pair),
crossing capacity $\{\text{small}, \text{medium}, \text{large}\}$ with the same
epoch grid $n\in\{1,2,4,10,20,40\}$ at 3 seeds and the same $B{=}20$M token budget,
under AdamW. $R_{\mathrm{free}}=20$ at every capacity (per-seed $\{40,20,20\}$
small, $\{20,20,20\}$ medium, $\{20,20,20\}$ large; Table~\ref{tab:pcfg}), with a
sharp crossing at the boundary: excess is $0.010$--$0.013$ nats at $n=20$ across all
three capacities, jumping to $0.064$/$0.130$/$0.249$ nats at $n=40$
(small/medium/large)---nothing lands near the threshold. This answers the outlook
question of \S\ref{sec:discussion}: the $4$--$10$ band's numeric value is
Markov-specific (PCFG tolerates ${\sim}2$--$5\times$ more repetition before
breaking), but the qualitative law---flat excess, then a sharp, capacity-invariant
onset---reproduces on a second generative family, and the linear-ratio account
has its capacity leg falsified on both families (onset location does not move with capacity on
either one); PCFG's single entropy setting leaves the entropy leg of the ratio untested
there. In short: the onset structure is portable across generative families; the
numeric constant is family-specific. A secondary observation, not one of the preregistered claims: severity
at $n=40$ grows monotonically with capacity ($0.064<0.130<0.249$, a ${>}4\times$
spread), a capacity gradient the Markov P2 sweep did not show; we flag it as a
follow-up question rather than a claim.

\paragraph{An optimizer swap (Muon).}
On the Markov medium-entropy cell, we swap AdamW for Muon \citep{jordan2024muon} at

memorization-clock account in \S\ref{sec:p3} and \S\ref{sec:memclock}. This contrasts with a companion optimizer-clock finding on a
different testbed, where Muon trivializes an objective-driven grokking clock---leaving
no objective-dependent delay to shift---rather than re-shaping it; taken together,
optimizer effects on training-dynamics clocks are
clock-specific, not a single ``Muon removes clocks'' rule.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Data-repetition and repeated-data scaling laws.}
\citet{muennighoff2023scaling} fit the repetition value-decay law and the $\approx
4$-epoch constant at LLM scale; we examine its universality at a scale orders of
magnitude smaller with independently tunable entropy and capacity. \citet{hernandez2022repeateddata} develop
scaling laws for repeated data and predict non-monotone (double-descent) loss curves under
some repetition regimes; we do not observe such non-monotone decay on our grid.
\citet{charton2024repeated} report that repeated examples can in fact beat single-use data
on certain mathematical tasks, showing that repetition is not uniformly harmful---context
that sharpens the question of when the threshold is crossed. The Chinchilla scaling
study \citep{hoffmann2022chinchilla} established compute-optimal data-scaling recipes that
underlie modern pretraining norms; our work is downstream of that setting, examining what
happens after the token budget is fixed and unique data become scarce. The original
neural scaling laws \citep{kaplan2020scalinglaws} provide the power-law backdrop against
which data-constrained regimes are defined. \citet{sorscher2022beyond} showed that data
pruning can yield super-power-law scaling, pointing to the quality dimension of data value;
our results address the repetition (quantity/novelty) dimension.

\paragraph{Quantitative comparison with the Muennighoff et al.\ benchmark.}
Table~\ref{tab:prior-work} places our results against the closest large-scale prior-work anchor
in explicit numerical terms.
\citet{muennighoff2023scaling} measured $R_{\mathrm{free}} \approx 4$ epochs at
LLM scale (billion-parameter models, billion-token corpora of natural language).
Our experiments find a lower bound of $R_{\mathrm{free}} \approx 4$ across all
nine grid cells, with the small-capacity corner extending to $R_{\mathrm{free}} = 10$
at parameter scales $150$--$3{,}500\times$ smaller and token budgets
$18{,}000$--$45{,}000\times$ smaller ($2.5$--$57$M params, $20$--$50$M tokens).
The low-end quantitative agreement ($R_{\mathrm{free}} \geq 4$ at our scale) is the
primary result: the same $\approx 4$-epoch free band reappears orders of magnitude smaller,
with no sign of the ${\sim}100\times$ upward shift in $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ that a
strong capacity-scaling reading of the large-scale result would predict
(Fig.~\ref{fig:scale-band}).
This is a test, not a replication: our setting differs in corpus type (synthetic
Markov vs.\ natural language), model scale, and tokenisation, so the match is
evidence for bounded universality rather than a controlled reproduction.
\citet{hernandez2022repeateddata} and \citet{charton2024repeated} predict qualitatively
similar value-decay shapes (free band then sharp decline); in their worst case, repeating
$0.1\%$ of the data $100$ times degraded an $800$M-parameter model to the performance of a
$400$M-parameter one (a ${\approx}2\times$ effective-size loss via mid-training double
descent). Our value-decay curves are
consistent in shape at toy scale but show no such non-monotone double descent, because we
repeat the whole corpus rather than a small, heavily-repeated fraction.

\paragraph{The free band is a scale-dependent floor, not a universal constant.}
The published anchors describe a floor rather than a fixed value.
\citet{muennighoff2023scaling} fit a repeated-token half-life of $R^{*}_{D}\approx15$
(${\approx}16$ epochs, at which a repeated token retains $1-1/e\approx0.63$ of its value)
sitting above their $\approx4$-epoch ``nearly free'' band, across models up to $8.7$B
parameters and $900$B tokens. \citet{yan2025repeatmore} sharpen this into a scaling
statement: the tolerable repetition count is not constant but grows---$\Theta(\log N)$ in the
strongly-convex case---with dataset size $N$. Our grid carries the same qualitative lesson on
a different axis: $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ is not a single number but ranges from $4$ to $10$ with
model capacity and source entropy, holding its $\approx4$-epoch lower bound across more than four
orders of magnitude of model scale (from our $2.5$--$57$M-parameter synthetic and
${\sim}5$M-parameter byte models up to the $8.7$B--$120$B-parameter LLMs of
\citet{muennighoff2023scaling,taylor2022galactica}) while its upper edge drifts to $10$ in the small-capacity,
low-entropy corner. Whether the controlling variable is dataset size
\citep{yan2025repeatmore}, model capacity, source entropy, or mixture composition
\citep{sedova2026mixture}, the published and present
evidence converge on one conclusion: ``${\approx}4$ free epochs'' is a robust
lower bound that holds at toy scale, not a universal constant.

\paragraph{Memorization and generalization.}
The interplay of memorization and generalisation is central to our P3 finding.
\citet{tirumala2022memorization} show that large LLMs memorize training data faster and
earlier in training, and that memorization and overfitting can decouple---motivating our
use of a copied-canary probe to detect the onset of harmful memorization; at scale this
onset is itself emergent yet predictable from smaller models \citep{biderman2023emergentmem}.
\citet{carlini2021extracting} demonstrate --- and \citet{carlini2023quantifying}
quantify across model scales --- that verbatim training sequences are extractable
from language models, establishing memorization as a concrete, measurable phenomenon.
\citet{feldman2020longtail} provide theoretical justification for why memorization of
rare examples can be necessary for optimal generalisation, adding nuance to the view that
memorization is purely harmful. These works frame our finding---that
repetition decay onset coincides with canary-memorization onset---as a specific instance
of the broader memorization-generalisation transition in neural-network learning.

\paragraph{Toy-model and small-scale science.}
Using controlled small-scale testbeds to isolate scaling-law
mechanisms follows a productive tradition. \citet{power2022grokking} introduced grokking on
small algorithmic datasets as a window into delayed generalisation, a phenomenon later shown
to extend beyond algorithmic data \citep{liu2023omnigrok}.
\citet{wortsman2023smallscale} demonstrated that small-scale proxy experiments can predict
large-scale transformer training instabilities, validating the approach of stress-testing
at reduced scale before committing to LLM-scale experiments. The Pythia model suite
\citep{biderman2023pythia} provides a complementary strategy---tracking training dynamics
across 70M--12B parameters on fixed public data---that could serve as a medium-scale bridge
between our synthetic Markov setting and full LLM-scale pretraining. A related
grokking $\times$ generalization-bounds analysis \citep{neucom2026grokking} shares the same
small-controlled-testbed methodology while addressing a different phenomenon (delayed
generalisation on algorithmic tasks); a further Neurocomputing study accelerating
grokking on arithmetic tasks via Kolmogorov--Arnold representations
\citep{park2025kagrokking} is a third small-scale training-dynamics neighbour on a
distinct phenomenon.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

A practically used repetition heuristic from large-scale training has a bounded analogue at
small scale: the ${\approx}4$-epoch free band survives our capacity/entropy-artifact
falsification test at a scale and in a data regime far removed from where it was fitted.
The coincidence of decay onset with memorization onset gives a mechanistic reading
consistent with the large-scale account (repetition becomes harmful once the model begins
to memorize repeated tokens \citep{tirumala2022memorization}). The result is positive but
bounded: it strengthens the case for treating a $4$--$10$ free-epoch band as a useful
small-scale default, while leaving exact thresholds and LLM-scale transfer unresolved
(Table~\ref{tab:evidence-ledger} collects each claim, its diagnostic, and its boundary).

\paragraph{Relation to the data-value literature.}
The repetition dimension studied here complements the data-quality dimension of
\citet{sorscher2022beyond} and the memorization view of \citet{feldman2020longtail}
(both framed in \S\ref{sec:related}): even high-quality tokens lose value after
$\approx 4$--$10$ repetitions, with the model transitioning from learning structure to
memorizing surface statistics as $n$ increases. This reading---explaining the band by an
established memorization mechanism rather than a new capacity--entropy law
(\S\ref{sec:memclock})---follows the reconciliation register of recent in-venue work
\citep{neucom2026grokking}.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}
The current testbed has two hard limits. First, it now uses two synthetic data
families (Markov and PCFG corpora, \S\ref{sec:pcfgmuon}) at one token budget (20M
tokens for the core sweep); the post-threshold decay shape is now measured on a
fine epoch grid in four cells (\S\ref{sec:p4}); the remaining five cells now have
measured threshold crossings (Table~\ref{tab:crossings}) but only two fine rungs
each ($n\in\{6,8\}$), so their decay shape and all other budgets remain unprobed,
and the fitted log--log slopes ($1.7$--$2.9$,
window-sensitive) are descriptive, not a law. The PCFG addition carries its own
caveats: the copied-canary probe is broken at the PCFG fresh-data baseline (median
gap $0.094$--$0.110$ nats at $n{=}1$, at or above the $0.10$-nat threshold, with
seed-structured variation), so no decay--memorization-coincidence claim is made for
that family; the Muon optimizer arm covers only the small and medium capacities at
one entropy level (no large-capacity cell); and both new arms run 3 seeds per cell,
without the bootstrap-scale confidence intervals Appendix~\ref{app:bootstrap} gives
the two Markov boundary cells. The Muon arm also inverts the decay/memorization
onset ordering at medium capacity, so the memorization-clock reading of
\S\ref{sec:memclock} is established for AdamW and does not transfer to Muon
unmodified. Second,
entropy is
construction-controlled rather than measured on natural text, so the mapping to
natural-language perplexity scales is indirect. Relatedly, the band's numeric label
is convention-dependent at the small-capacity corner: under the relative
$\varepsilon = \phi H$ criterion (Table~\ref{tab:twoconv}) it reads $2$--$10$
rather than $4$--$10$, with seven of nine cell verdicts and every headline
conclusion (bounded band, no ratio-driven shift) unchanged. The onset-ordering
claim is likewise detection-threshold-scoped: it survives canary thresholds at or
below the declared $0.10$ nats but inverts in six of nine cells at $0.20$ nats
(\S\ref{sec:p3}); on real bytes it is additionally corpus-scoped, since the
planted-canary clock lags decay on the duplication-heavy code corpus at $30$M
parameters (\S\ref{sec:realscale}). It is also scoped to the probe convention and to
the axis on which onsets are counted, and this is the sharpest limit on it: a
$324$-run exposure-matched control (\S\ref{sec:p3exposure}) leaves
$R_{\mathrm{free}}$ identical in all nine cells but moves the nominal ordering from
coincides to lags in six of them at plant rate $1$, and on the gradient-exposure
axis the ordering reads lags in all nine cells at both plant rates. The band and the
P2 falsification are therefore convention-robust, while leads-or-coincides is a
statement about the nominal per-epoch axis under a $4\times$-oversampled probe, and
the memorization-clock reading of \S\ref{sec:memclock} is correspondingly a
nominal-axis heuristic rather than a demonstrated mechanism: its
$R_{\mathrm{free}} \approx k^\star$ agreement holds in epochs and becomes a
$5$--$10\times$ gap in gradient exposures. The capacity axis is fully covered---all three large-capacity cells span
the full epoch grid $n\in\{1,2,4,10,20,40\}$ (\S\ref{sec:largecompletion}), and the
xl/xxl extension stretches the span to $\sim 23\times$ ($2.5$M--$57$M params;
\S\ref{sec:capxl})---but even $57$M params
remains modest relative to LLM scale, and the extension has no fresh $n{=}1$ arm
(its excess is referenced to $n{=}2$, a bias bounded at $0.008$ nats on the main grid). The
real-text bridge now includes five $50$M WikiText seeds, three smaller $10$M audit
seeds, a five-seed source-code arm, and a 48-run FineWeb-Edu/GitHub-code
replication at $30$M and $57$M parameters (\S\ref{sec:realscale}), so real text
now reaches the same $57$M ceiling as the synthetic grid; but the replication runs
two seeds per cell, covers two corpus families with no deduplication arm, and
stops below ${\sim}100$M parameters---it rules out Markov-specific and
small-model artifacts but does not prove LLM-scale robustness. The code arm's two original caveats have
been converted into tests: the canary probe is now ported to real bytes (with the
bank-exchangeability convention stated in \S\ref{sec:scale}), and near-duplication was
tested directly and identified as the driver of the as-collected contraction. What
remains open there: a single code source family (one installation's Python), the
threshold-edge third deduplicated draw, and the sensitivity of absolute real-byte
canary levels to bank composition. Separately, a learning-rate (lr) sweep on the med/med
cell ($10^{-4}$, $3\times10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$; within-lr fresh baselines; fine epoch
grid) keeps $R_{\mathrm{free}}$ within $6$--$8$---inside the $4$--$10$ band, moving at
most one fine rung across a $10\times$ lr span---so the band is not an artifact of the
single default learning rate, though a full lr$\times$cell factorial and a second
architecture remain untested. The optimizer sweep is asymmetric in one respect we
state plainly: weight decay is fixed at $0.01$ in every run of the study
(\S\ref{sec:setup}) and is never varied, so every claim here is a
single-weight-decay claim, and the regularization axis has not been probed the way
the learning-rate axis has. A weight-decay robustness arm on the same design
(rates $0$, $0.01$, $0.1$, $0.3$) is specified and its runner built, but it is not
part of the evidence reported here. The Pythia suite \citep{biderman2023pythia}, which tracks training dynamics
across a 70M--12B parameter range on public data, would be a natural platform for a future
medium-scale bridge experiment between our setting and full LLM scale.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Three directions are most consequential. First, the capacity-span question is now
answered within this study's scale: the xl/xxl extension (\S\ref{sec:capxl}) holds the
band at $4$--$10$ out to $57$M params (${\sim}23\times$), and the real-text
replication (\S\ref{sec:realscale}) keeps FineWeb-Edu inside the band at the same
$57$M ceiling, so what remains is the jump to genuinely LLM-scale models rather
than a wider span at this scale.
Second, the non-Markov extension is answered rather than merely
proposed: a PCFG corpus reproduces the qualitative law---a
flat excess band followed by a sharp, capacity-invariant onset---but at a family-specific
value ($R_{\mathrm{free}}=20$ across all three capacities, versus $4$--$10$ for Markov;
\S\ref{sec:pcfgmuon}), confirming that the onset structure, not the numeric band, is the
invariant object. What remains open is an entropy-matched comparison across families (the
two corpora's measured entropies, $2.54$ vs.\ $4.04$ bits, are constructed independently
rather than dialed to coincide) and a further test on hierarchical or
natural-language-adjacent grammars richer than either testbed used here. Third, a
medium-scale Pythia-style bridge at 100M--1B parameters would quantify how much of the
LLM-scale gap between our setting and \citet{muennighoff2023scaling} is driven by scale
versus corpus statistics. Until those studies exist, the present finding is best read as:
the repetition value-decay law is not obviously a capacity/entropy artifact at
orders-of-magnitude smaller scale, the onset mechanism is consistent across scales and
across a second generative family, and the exact four-epoch constant is not universal in
the small-capacity corner or across generative families.

\section{Case study: the decay onset in one corpus cell}\label{sec:casestudy}

To make the aggregate result concrete, we trace one medium-capacity,
medium-entropy (``med/med'') cell across all epoch counts. The key event is the
cliff: a sharp rise in excess validation loss once repetition passes
$R_{\mathrm{free}}$. Its fresh-budget reference loss is $1.764$~nats.

The excess stays below $0.05$ nats through $n = 4$ (values: $0.000$, $0.003$, $0.014$),
then breaks sharply at $n = 10$ ($0.136$), climbs to $1.645$ at $n = 20$, and reaches

$n = 10$, exactly coinciding with the decay onset---writing $\delta_{\mathrm{decay}}$ and
$\delta_{\mathrm{mem}}$ for the decay-onset and memorization-onset epochs (the smallest $n$
exceeding each threshold), we have $\delta_{\mathrm{decay}} = \delta_{\mathrm{mem}} = 10$.
This single cell exemplifies the \S\ref{sec:p1} aggregate
finding: the repetition cliff is sharp, the free zone ends at $n = 4$, and decay onset
coincides with memorization onset. Within a single run the same transition shows up as a
training--validation split: a free run ($n=4$) keeps training and validation loss locked
together, whereas a damaged run ($n=20$) drives training loss down (memorizing the reused
tokens) while validation loss climbs (Fig.~\ref{fig:within-run}, Appendix~\ref{app:within-run}).



\section{Within-run divergence trajectory}\label{app:within-run}

Figure~\ref{fig:within-run} traces a single large-capacity, medium-entropy run at each side
of the free band and shows that the aggregate cliff has a within-run signature: a run inside
the free band ($n=4$) keeps training and validation loss together for its whole schedule,
whereas a run past the band ($n=20$) splits them---training loss falls as the model memorizes
the repeated tokens while validation loss rises. The split begins around the fourth epoch,
locating when within a run repetition turns harmful.



\section{Bootstrap audit of the boundary cells}\label{app:bootstrap}

The small/low and small/med cells sit at a median excess of $0.0498$ nats at $n=10$ on
the original five seeds, $0.0002$ nats below the $0.05$-nat free threshold, so the upper
edge of the $4$--$10$ band turns on the finest digit of one statistic. A seed-level
nonparametric bootstrap ($10^{4}$ resamples of the paper's statistic, resampling both
the repetition seeds and the three fresh-baseline seeds; the statistic is the median final
validation loss at $n{=}10$ minus the median fresh baseline at $n{=}1$ over three seeds)
on those five seeds gave threshold-straddling 95\% CIs ($[0.030,0.063]$ and
$[0.044,0.063]$; exceedance probabilities $0.34$ and $0.50$). We then ran the direct
test of whether this indeterminacy is sampling noise: ten additional seeds per cell,
tripling the budget to 15. The intervals narrow onto the threshold rather than away
from it. For small/low the median excess is $0.0447$ nats with 95\% CI
$[0.029, 0.057]$ and exceedance probability $0.15$ (the cell leans free at $n{=}10$);
for small/med the median is $0.0492$ nats with 95\% CI $[0.043, 0.062]$ and exceedance
probability $0.42$ (the cell sits on the threshold). The residual indeterminacy is
therefore a property of the cells---their $n{=}10$ excess genuinely lies at the
threshold---not of the seed budget. This is why Section~\ref{sec:p1} reports
$R_{\mathrm{free}}$ as a $4$--$10$ band under a stated threshold rather than a point
constant. A finer epoch grid brackets both cells' onsets directly: median excess at
$n=6/8/12/16$ is $0.019/0.040/0.074/0.135$ for small/low and $0.014/0.026/0.077/0.180$
for small/med (ten seeds per point), against $0.045/0.049$ at $n=10$ (15 seeds) and
$0.231/0.391$ at $n=20$ (15 seeds). Small/med therefore crosses the decay threshold at
$n\approx10$--$11$; small/low between $n=10$ and $n=12$. The same ladder adjudicates the
one onset-coincidence miss of \S\ref{sec:p3}: in small/low the canary gap crosses its
$0.10$-nat threshold already at $n\approx8$ (median $0.111$, ten seeds) while decay
onsets at $n\approx11$, so the miss is a genuine, seed-robust dissociation in which
memorization leads decay by roughly half an octave---the ordering a memorization-gated
account requires (the gate must open before the cost appears), not a counterexample to
it. The $n=20$ ``broken'' verdicts rest on 15 seeds per cell; the $n=4$ ``free''
verdicts on five. The band edges, by
contrast, are seed-stable: at $n=4$ every seed of both cells is free (maximum per-seed
excess $0.021$ nats, half the threshold) and at $n=20$ every seed has broken (minimum
per-seed excess $0.214$ nats, four times the threshold)---only the $n=10$ interior of the
band is indeterminate. A revision-round audit extends the same seed-robustness test to
the third boundary rung, med/low at $n{=}10$, whose over-threshold verdict rested on
three seeds (median excess $+0.078$): on twelve fresh seeds (seeds 20--31, same 20M-token
budget) all twelve land above the $0.05$-nat threshold, with median excess $+0.073$
(per-seed range $+0.068$ to $+0.101$), so the med/low onset at $n{=}10$ is seed-robust,
not a three-seed accident. The revision fine grid (Table~\ref{tab:crossings}) also
re-measures the two small-capacity cells' $n{=}8$ rungs on three fresh seeds,
reproducing the archived ten-seed medians ($0.034$ vs.\ $0.040$ for small/low and
$0.026$ vs.\ $0.026$ for small/med). The $8/9$ decay-onset/memorization-onset coincidence rate of
Section~\ref{sec:p3} carries a bootstrap 95\% CI of $[0.67, 1.00]$: strong evidence the
coincidence is the rule, with one honest miss. (The bootstrap script is included in the
archived code repository; see Data availability.)
